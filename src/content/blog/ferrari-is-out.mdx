[`v0.5.0-ferrari`](https://github.com/fdimitri/carbide2/releases/tag/v0.5.0-ferrari)
is tagged. The last release made the agent and the workspace usable. This one is
the layer underneath: who may say who they are, and whether a workspace can be
changed without a `kubectl patch`.

A token used to arrive in a URL and be minted by two different services under
two different names for the same secret. Now it doesn't. On top of that fixed
handshake sit passkeys and a workspace-configuration surface.

## The hard cutover

For its whole life the worker took its JWT in the WebSocket URL, which meant the
token landed in every access log between the browser and the pod. [The dual path
that was supposed to ease the transition couldn't actually fall
back](/blog/the-fallback-we-decided-not-to-keep), and the answer was to delete
it: no `?token=` shim, no deprecation window, first frame is `system/auth`. The
token travels in a frame, which access logs never see.

## Control mints, pods verify

[The token's constitution](/docs/design/decisions/adr-015-authentication-token-ownership/)
is ADR-015: control is the sole minter, workspaces verify and never mint, and
signing is asymmetric so a pod that can check a token can't forge one. The
server-minted path and the shared secret came out. Identity stopped being
pod-local integers and [became typed UUIDs](/blog/uuids-everywhere) — same
reason every id collision this release happened, wearing a PEM or a number.

## Passkeys

Passkeys shipped as a second credential on the login screen. Username-first and
non-resident — the shape that works on a YubiKey without eating one of its
limited resident slots. The control side is `webauthn-ruby` plus two small
tables (a credential record and a one-time challenge) and two ceremonies:
register a key, then assert it. The client gets a passkey button on login and a
management surface in the control settings to add or remove keys.

The part that matters is what passkeys don't change. They terminate in the same
login token 015 just rebuilt, so none of the workspace-token, worker-auth, or
handshake work downstream knows the difference. One more way to get the token;
zero new token shapes.

## Workspace shape gets a surface

A workspace stopped being something you hand-patch on the cluster. [The shape,
the surface, and the rough way to configure it](/blog/workspace-shape-surface-config)
have their own post. Short version: patch vs migration, `PATCH` vs `roll`, and a
config modal that's one-workspace-at-a-time.

## The four ADRs drawing the forward arc

**[ADR-019](/docs/design/decisions/adr-019-commandset-caps-versioning/)** retires
the wire-protocol integer for per-function SemVer capabilities. Major is the only
thing compared.

**[ADR-020](/docs/design/decisions/adr-020-decider-capability-resolution/)** makes
the Decider real: two capsets compared into three arrays, graded into four
states. The picker selects a grade, not a pile of SHAs.

**[ADR-022](/docs/design/decisions/adr-022-adversarial-agent-agenda/)** proposes
making the "second model told to find defects" trick a first-class thing. Least
implementable, possibly most useful.

**[ADR-024](/docs/design/decisions/adr-024-multi-stream-worker/)** splits the
single worker socket into per-domain sockets so a blocked agent conversation
can't stall terminals and chat.

## The line in the sand

Still `0.x`. The capability system exists on paper and in the first registries,
but the emitter half and the Decider's grade math aren't wired. The worker still
multiplexes one socket. The authorization model — ADR-017 — is the next big
thing, deliberately deferred to 0.6.0.

What changed is the foundation: one minting authority, one handshake, one token
format, a second credential, and a workspace that configures itself from its own
API instead of a kubectl patch.
