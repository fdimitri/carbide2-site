Ferrari is the release where authentication stopped being two parallel designs
and became one. The fallback got its own post; this is the one underneath it.
[ADR-015](/docs/design/decisions/adr-015-authentication-token-ownership/) is the
token's constitution, and the interesting thing about it is how much of it is
deletion.

## One issuer, zero mints in the pod

The old arrangement had two services minting JWTs under two different names for
what was supposed to be the same secret, and a worker that could verify a token
and, because it held the same shared secret, also *make* one. A pod that could
check a token could forge one for every workspace. That's a consequence of
symmetric signing, where verifying and minting are the same operation — not a
defect in any one flow.

Control is now the sole minter. The workspace server's `WorkerTokenIssuer` is
gone, the `ws_token` endpoint is gone, the legacy format branch in the worker is
gone. One issuer, one format, one place to reason about who is allowed to say
who they are.

## Signing went asymmetric

The shared secret became an RSA keypair. Control holds the private half; every
pod verifies against a public JWKS endpoint it fetches and caches. A pod can
prove a token is real without ever being able to make one.

That's the whole security argument in one sentence: verification and minting
stopped being the same privilege. The keypair itself joined the deploy path —
`deploy.rb` mints it once and stores it as a Kubernetes Secret, and the public
half is what gets published. The signing key is the one thing that stays a
Secret rather than a settings column, because a key that can mint is not
configuration in the same sense as a TTL.

## Identity got typed

The token's subject and audience became `user:<uuid>` and `workspace:<uuid>`.
There's a whole post about why that happened; the short version is two databases
both calling a column `user_id` and meaning different things. The integers left
the token in the same window as the handshake cutover, because there was no
installed base to stage for.

## The ceiling

Login tokens renew without a password, but not forever. Renewal preserves
`auth_time` — the original login moment — and re-signs a fresh expiry. Once
`now - auth_time` passes seven days, renewal is refused and the only path is a
full login. Sliding, but bounded.

The TTL stopped being an env var and became a settings column, so TTL and the
ceiling change without a redeploy. That's a real control-plane surface now, not
a rename: a `Setting` model, a settings API, a pane in the dashboard.

## What's left

The authorization model — who may patch a workspace, who may read what — is the
next layer and it is deliberately not this one. ADR-015 gives the token a stable
identity and a single minting authority, which is exactly the property every
future authorization decision needs to lean on. It doesn't decide what anyone is
allowed to do; it decides who they are, and who's allowed to say it.
